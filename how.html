How it works

Grassroots Contracts are apps that belong to the people who use them. Each starts as a few rules in plain English and is turned, step by step, into an app that runs on people's own phones.

1 · Ownership

Your phone, your rules

Most apps live on a company's servers. The company keeps the data, sets the rules, and can change them or shut the app down. A grassroots app has no such center: each person's phone runs its own copy, keeps its own data, and talks directly to the phones of the people it deals with.

2 · Grassroots

Start anywhere, join up later

A grassroots app needs no headquarters and no permission. A family, a club or a village can start using one on its own. Somewhere else, another group does the same. When people from the two groups meet and agree, the two become one larger group; nothing has to be moved or migrated.

The idea is old. In 1863, Proudhon described federation as a pact between families, towns and groups in which each party keeps more than it gives up. The mathematical definition of a grassroots platform says the same about apps: many copies that start independently of one another, and that join into larger ones once their people connect.

3 · Contracts

Who may do what, and whose yes it needs

Every app here starts from a contract: a few rules in plain English. This is the whole contract of the friendship app. (It builds on a smaller one, about having a key and getting to know people.)

◆
FriendshipAlice and Bob, who know each other personally and are not friends, may jointly decide to become friends.
■
Ending a friendshipAlice may end their friendship with Bob.

Each rule becomes a speech act: an action, the people taking part, and whose agreement it needs. A question mark after a name means that person must say yes. Becoming friends needs two yeses. Ending a friendship needs one: Bob is not asked. Try it:

befriend(Alice?, Bob?)

An app can check some rules and not others. It cannot know whether Alice and Bob really met. So each rule is one of three kinds:

■

Enforced

The app makes it so.

Ending a friendship always works.
◆

Attested

You sign that it is true.

"We know each other personally."
○

Undertaken

A promise the app cannot check.

Standing behind everything signed with your key.

As with any contract, make it with people you trust to keep their word.

4 · By algorithm

From words to app, step by step

Nobody writes these apps by hand. A chain of programs turns the English contract into the app. AI helps at exactly one step: rewriting the English rules as speech acts, which are short enough for a person to read and check. Every step after that is an ordinary program, which gives the same output for the same input.

One of these programs is a checker. It asks three things of the speech acts: that some act can start everything from nothing, that everything a person holds was put there by some act, and that every act is wanted by someone. A theorem says that when all three hold, the app is grassroots. So that property is not hoped for: it is checked by a program and guaranteed by the theorem, by design.
